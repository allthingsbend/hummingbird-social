A birth plan is not really a plan. It is a short summary of what matters to you, written down while you are calm, so that other people can read it quickly when you are not.

The problem with most templates is length. They run five pages, cover every conceivable scenario, and never get read by the person who needs to read it. This one is built the other way around: detailed worksheets for you to think it through, and a single clean page to actually hand over.

## What's inside

- **A one-page summary** — the version you print several copies of and bring with you
- **Worksheets** for working out your preferences before you condense them: environment, movement and positions, pain management, monitoring
- **Support people** page — who is there, what each person is doing, who is not
- **If plans change** — preferences around a cesarean, an induction, or a transfer
- **Immediately after birth** — skin to skin, cord clamping, feeding preferences
- **Newborn care preferences**
- **Questions for your provider**, so you arrive at your appointment with them written down
- **Hospital bag checklist**

## How to use it

Fill in the worksheets over a few sittings, then condense the answers onto the one-page summary. Bring that page to an appointment and talk through it with your provider before you finalize it. Print four or five copies — one for your bag, one for your partner, and spares for whoever is in the room.

## A note on scope

This is an organizational template, not medical guidance. It does not tell you what to choose or what is safest. Every preference here is a starting point for a conversation with the provider who knows your history.

## Formats and printing

US Letter and A4, standard and fillable. The fillable version is the practical one here — type it, revise it, print the final summary.
