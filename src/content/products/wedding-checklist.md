Wedding planning goes wrong in a predictable way. Nothing is hard on its own, but there are roughly two hundred small decisions, they depend on each other, and they arrive out of order. You book a venue in March and then realize in September that the invitations needed to go out in July.

This checklist fixes the ordering problem. It is a full wedding planner laid out as a twelve-month timeline, so every task sits in the month it actually needs to happen rather than in one long undated list.

## What's inside

- **A 12-month timeline**, broken into month-by-month task pages from twelve months out through the week after the wedding
- **Budget worksheet** with the standard categories and space for your estimated, actual, and remaining amounts
- **Vendor tracker** — contact details, contract status, deposit paid, balance due, final payment date
- **Guest list and RSVP tracker**, counted by household rather than by person
- **Stationery timeline** covering save the dates, invitations, RSVP deadline, and day-of pieces
- **Day-of schedule** template, from hair and makeup call time through last dance
- **Emergency kit and packing lists** for the getting-ready room

## Who it's for

Couples planning it themselves without a full-service planner, and anyone helping — a maid of honor, a mother of the bride, a friend who volunteered and now needs a system.

It is deliberately not a scrapbook. There are no inspiration collage pages or quote pages padding out the count. Every page is something you fill in and act on.

## Formats and printing

You get four files: US Letter and A4, each in a standard version and a fillable version. Print the whole thing, or print the sections you want and add them to a binder as you go.

The pages are designed for plain home-printer paper. If you want something sturdier for a binder, 32 lb paper holds up well without needing a print shop. Our [printing guide](/printing-guide/) covers paper weights in more detail.
