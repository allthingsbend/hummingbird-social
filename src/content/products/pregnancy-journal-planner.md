Pregnancy generates two completely different kinds of paper. There is the sentimental kind — the first ultrasound, the week you felt movement, the name list you kept changing. And there is the administrative kind — appointment dates, test results, questions you meant to ask and forgot, a registry, a hospital bag.

Most products pick one and ignore the other. This one carries both, interleaved, so you are keeping one book instead of two.

## What's inside

- **Week-by-week pages** from week four to week forty-two, with space for how you felt, what changed, and a photo
- **Appointment log** — date, provider, what was discussed, what comes next
- **Questions for my provider** pages, so they are written down before you are in the room
- **Symptom and energy tracker**
- **Name lists**, with columns for both partners' opinions
- **Registry planning** pages by category
- **Nursery planning** and setup checklist
- **Hospital bag checklist** for you, your partner, and the baby
- **Birth preferences summary** page
- **Letters to the baby** pages

## Who it's for

First-time parents mostly, though the trackers hold up fine for a second or third pregnancy when you already know the routine and just want the appointments in one place.

## A note on scope

This is a journal and organizer. It does not include medical guidance, symptom interpretation, or advice about what is normal. Anything health-related belongs with your provider.

## Formats and printing

Four files — US Letter and A4, standard and fillable. At sixty-plus pages this is the one most people take to a print shop and have spiral bound, though a three-ring binder works just as well and lets you add pages.
